Reconnaissance Is Just Market Validation With a Different Objective

This past Friday, I was fortunate to attend my first TIGER Talk, hosted by Innovate New Albany, where a speaker from Rev1 Ventures discussed Market Validation & Feedback. In a room full of startup leaders and growing business owners, we explored the importance of collecting qualitative and quantitative feedback, researching different target audiences, mitigating bias in our findings, and ultimately applying lean startup and validated learning principles to ensure that the products and services we're building actually solve problems customers want addressed.

While I've been developing my own market validation and feedback strategies this week, and applying lean startup methods to launching my own endeavors, I've noticed an interesting pattern: much of this process closely resembles something I know quite well from my work in offensive security.

Reconnaissance.

What are attackers learning about your business?

Understanding Your Target Before Building Your Solution

Founders use research and feedback to answer questions about their potential customers and what they need to build, like:

  • Who is my target customer?
  • What does my target customer do?
  • What products and services do they currently use?
  • Where might opportunities for my product or service exist?

Attackers use reconnaissance to answer questions about their target:

  • Who are they? (Who is my target customer?)
  • What do they do? (What does my target customer do?)
  • What technology do they use? (What products and services do they currently use?)
  • Where might opportunities for me to gain access exist? (Where might opportunities for my product or service exist?)

The questions are ultimately the same. They both gather intelligence to make the decisions, but the underlying intent and lens in which the questions are asked are very different.

Intelligence Drives Decisions

For businesses, intelligence gathered through market validation and feedback helps determine what to build, what needs to change, and what isn't worth pursuing. Not everything that can be built should be, and not everything already built necessarily meets customer needs. Data-informed decisions help businesses focus their resources on solving the right problems and delivering value to customers.

For attackers, reconnaissance provides the intelligence needed to identify potential weaknesses and determine which opportunities are worth pursuing. Not every system, process, or technology represents a viable target. Understanding the environment helps attackers identify potential entry points and prioritize their efforts toward achieving their objectives.

The underlying principle is the same: information is only valuable when you understand what it means and how to act on it.

What should you do?

This concept of turning intelligence into actionable decisions applies not only to how you grow your business, but also to how you protect it.

Hexpo helps organizations understand their security exposure by bringing together information about assets, vulnerabilities, and findings so they can identify what matters and prioritize where to focus their efforts.

SitRep takes threat intelligence and turns it into a concise, actionable picture of the threat landscape, helping organizations understand relevant threats and make more informed security decisions.

Both are built around the same principle: understand your environment, identify what matters, and act on that intelligence.

Attackers are already looking for ways into your business. You should be just as intentional about understanding where you're exposed, what matters most, and what you need to do about it.
