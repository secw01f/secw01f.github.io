Leaning into Superpowers

Lets talk about your superpowers

Personally, I am a firm believer that leaning into your strengths is going to get you far more wins than being hyper focused on trying to make your weaknesses, well... less weak. While we do have to give our weaknesses some love sometimes, (like being more outwardly communicative with posts like these, in my case), our superpowers are what move us forward. These are the things that we do naturally and not necessarily the things we practice the most.

My superpower

After a good amount of reflection, one of my superpower is finding problems in everyday life and recognizing opportunities to solve them with technology.

I am building a security advisory business along side several other SaaS ventures that I believe can provide flywheel affects for one another. Doing that means managing opportunities individually and collectively, experimenting with ideas, validating assumptions, and making informed decisions about where to invest my time.

So... I built E.V.A.N OS for myself.

But here's the distinction: my superpower isn't necessarily building the platform. It's recognizing the problem it solves and using it to make entrepreneurship more manageable, intentional, and sustainable.

This superpower has also shown up during one of the most difficult periods of my life.

Last year, my wife was diagnosed with breast cancer and underwent a double mastectomy at just 31 years old.

During her recovery, I found myself juggling a lot: tracking surgical drain output on sheets of paper multiple times a day, managing medication schedules, and navigating a mountain of medical instructions to make sure everything was being handled correctly.

There was very little room for error. Missing a medication, losing track of drainage output, or overlooking proper wound care could have serious consequences.

Having experienced that firsthand, I wouldn't wish that stress on anyone.

That's why I'm launching Ucovery! With the goal of making the recovery process easier for patients and their families through tools for drain tracking, medication management, and finding relevant recovery resources.

Once again, the same instinct: recognize a real-world problem and explore how technology can make it better.

*By the way, Abby (my wife) is doing amazing now and absolutely kicking ass at life. Love you honey!

Now I'm curious: What are your superpowers?

Take a moment to reflect.

What comes naturally to you? What do you find yourself doing differently from others? And more importantly, how can you lean into that strength to create your next win?

I'd genuinely love to hear what you come up with.

- Evan
